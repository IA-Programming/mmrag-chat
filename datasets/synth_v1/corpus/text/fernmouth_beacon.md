# Fernmouth Beacon

_A guide to the Fernmouth Beacon, the lighthouse of Duskwater in the Saltmere Archipelago._

## Overview

The Saltmere Archipelago is not short of curiosities, yet the lighthouse still stands out. What follows draws on parish records, interviews and the notebooks of several long-time residents of Eddersby. The lamp room of the Fernmouth Beacon is reached through a narrow trapdoor that tests the knees of every visitor. Salt spray has worn the lower courses of stone into soft, rounded shapes. Over the years the lighthouse has become something of a symbol for the whole archipelago. The keepers' cottages at the foot of the tower now house a small exhibition. Opinions about the lighthouse differ, but nobody on Duskwater is indifferent to it.

Fishing crews heading home use the Fernmouth Beacon as a reassurance as much as a guide. A brass logbook in the watch room of the Fernmouth Beacon records every ship that has passed within sight. Readers new to the islands should note that the place names here follow local spelling. It is fair to say that the lighthouse has shaped the rhythm of daily life in Fernmouth. Where sources disagree, the more cautious version has been preferred.

## History of the light

Several families from Fernmouth claim an ancestor who played a part in those years. During the hard winters that followed, progress slowed almost to a halt. After three winters of building work, the lamp of the Fernmouth Beacon was lit for the first time in 1900. Inside, the walls are lined with painted boards recording past keepers. On stormy nights the beam sweeps across the water in a slow, steady rhythm. The early accounts mention a sheltered wooden shed that served as office, store and kitchen at once. A sheltered letter preserved in the island archive complains about the cost of timber and rope.

## The tower

The fog signal is sounded only rarely these days, but the islanders still remember its voice. The soil is thin, and most gardens are sheltered behind hedges of gorse and hawthorn. Even on calm days the sound of the surf is never far away. In winter the moorland above Eddersby turns rough and quiet, and the wind rarely drops. The harbour at Eddersby is small but deep, and it has sheltered boats for generations.

A spiral staircase of 264 steps leads from the entrance hall to the lamp room of the Fernmouth Beacon. The light on Duskwater changes quickly, which has drawn painters to the island for a long time. Fields are small and irregular, shaped more by the rocks than by any plan. Peat cutting has left long dark scars across the moor, now softened by heather. Sudden hail can roll in from the west with very little warning. Most houses in Eddersby are built of the same stone, roofed with heavy slate. Seabirds nest in their thousands along the northern cliffs each autumn.

## Keepers and neighbours

Children from the outlying farms travel to school in Fernmouth by bus or, in bad weather, not at all. Local musicians gather in the back room of the harbour inn most weekends. Newcomers are usually accepted once they have survived their first autumn on the island. Shared work, from harvests to roof repairs, still binds the community together. A noticeboard outside the shop in Eddersby lists everything from lost dogs to choir rehearsals.

## The coast around Duskwater

Sheep graze right up to the cliff edge, apparently unconcerned by the drop. On a clear night the beam of the Fernmouth Beacon can be seen from 17 nautical miles away. Duskwater is a salt-stained island of low hills, stone walls and narrow lanes that end abruptly at the sea. From the high ground above Eddersby the neighbouring islands look close enough to touch on a clear day. A single road loops around the island, with tracks branching off towards the farms. Tidal pools along the shore are full of anemones, crabs and small darting fish. The coast here is broken into coves, sea stacks and shingle beaches that change shape after every storm. Several folk songs from Eddersby mention it, usually in the last verse.

A watercolour of the scene hangs in the reading room of the library in Fernmouth. The subject has inspired poems, paintings and at least one rather bad novel. It has been the subject of friendly rivalry between Eddersby and the neighbouring parishes. Not every story told about it is true, but most of them are entertaining. Some families keep scrapbooks of newspaper cuttings that mention it. There is even a recipe for barley bread that is said to have been named after it.

## Visiting

Guided walks leave from the visitor hut in Eddersby on most mornings in winter. Visitors should bring waterproof clothing whatever the forecast says. Opening arrangements can change at short notice, so it is wise to ask locally before setting out. Families with small children will find a sheltered picnic spot near the old quay. Accommodation on Duskwater is limited, and rooms fill up quickly in the busy season.

Photography is welcome, although drones are discouraged near nesting sites. Mobile phone coverage is patchy, especially on the far side of the hill. There is a small café nearby that serves crab soup and strong tea. The walk from the harbour at Eddersby is pleasant but steep in places. A short detour leads to a viewpoint with benches, a stone cairn and wide views over the bay. Those who prefer to explore alone can pick up a printed map at the harbour office. The best light for photographs is usually in the early morning or late evening.

## Stories and notes

Fishing crews know the Fernmouth Beacon by its single flash every 5 seconds. Amateur historians continue to argue about details that matter mostly to themselves. In Fernmouth it is considered bad luck to speak ill of it while at sea. Island children learn about it long before they can spell its name. A popular local saying holds that Duskwater has four seasons in a single afternoon. The island newsletter runs a short item about it at least once every summer. Visiting writers have described it as proud, a word the islanders have happily adopted.

Tea towels and postcards bearing its image are sold in the harbour shop. Older islanders insist that things were done more carefully in their day. Local schoolchildren sometimes choose it as the topic for their end-of-year projects. This guide collects what is known about the Fernmouth Beacon and its place in the life of Duskwater. Much of the material here was gathered during long autumn evenings in the reading room at Fernmouth.
